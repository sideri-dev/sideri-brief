Today

Memoket Gem proves an ambient-capture wearable can sell on hardware alone — no subscription, ¥31,980, in Japan.

Memoket Gem is an 11-gram Japanese wearable AI recorder: ¥31,980 with a wristband, 20 hours of continuous recording, IPX5-rated, and no subscription needed to use it — transcription is free up to 300 minutes a month, with paid tiers genuinely optional. Most ambient-capture wearables in this category (Plaud, Bee, Natura among them) subsidize the hardware and recoup on a subscription; this is real Japan-market evidence the category can clear at a real price on a one-time purchase. Independently confirmed by two separate Japanese outlets, with no caveats needed.

Nirva raises $25M for an ambient-capture pendant pitched on privacy, not price.

Nirva closed a $25M pre-Series A, bringing its total funding to $33M, to build a pendant CEO Wei Lyu says "only listens to your own speech" and which "by default doesn't keep any audio data or transcripts" after processing. At $249, orders open in November and shipping is promised before year-end — a new, well-funded entrant to the same ambient-capture category Memoket Gem (above) just undercut on business model. Two independent outlets confirm the funding figures without citing each other.

Bangkok's design expo now has real numbers behind it — and a second venue.

TCEB's government-organized Bangkok International Design Expo (November 25–29) has added hard targets: 6,000-plus attendees, 200-plus business-matching pairs, and an economic-impact goal above ฿280 million, plus a second venue, the Queen Sirikit National Convention Center, alongside the originally announced Bangkok Kunsthalle. A scaled, government-backed design event with a formal business-matching track. The official event site names only Bangkok Kunsthalle, read as partial coverage rather than a contradiction.

Japan's trade ministry is easing the paperwork on its overseas-seller import regime.

METI opened public comment, through November 24, on unifying the reporting deadline its "domestic manager" rule imposes on overseas-registered importers into a single annual snapshot, and letting one importer consolidate multiple product reports into one filing. The change doesn't touch an importer that sells through its own Japan-registered entity rather than this overseas-seller route — it's a signal the regime is getting less painful for the sellers who do use it, nothing more.

Yesterday's deep-dive

Raspberry Pi's November 2 price increase — $80 on the 16GB Raspberry Pi 5, to $385; $40 on the 16GB Compute Module 5 — lands on the one DRAM size its own compliance filings qualify zero alternative parts for, out of thirteen qualified parts across six vendors, none above 8GB per package. It breaks a price law that had held exactly — $45 plus $16.25 per gigabyte — across every other board size, pushing the 8-to-16GB step to $26.25 per gigabyte on both boards, reading as a straight component pass-through rather than a board reprice. The smaller board sizes were untouched this time, but the 2GB size moved 32 days earlier on the identical justification, so the pricing ladder is less settled than it looks. The announcement went out through a blog post rather than the company's regulated investor channel, and reached the Japanese market at two different prices at once as one reseller repriced overnight and another hadn't yet — a holiday-timing artifact, not a real decision either way.
