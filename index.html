Teenage Engineering opens its first permanent store — a 6,000 sq ft SoHo flagship built for community events, not transactions.

Teenage Engineering designed and built the space itself at 216 Lafayette Street in Manhattan, describing it as a venue for "launches, previews and get-togethers with the community" rather than a straightforward retail floor — the EP-133 K.O. II build line stays on the upper level for weeks after the launch, and the soft-opening featured a live Lykke Li performance cut straight to vinyl. It lands one week after founder Jesper Kouthoofd told press that hardware "isn't the business," which makes the choice of a permanent physical space for community and launch moments, rather than for sales volume, worth watching. Single-source so far — no second outlet has covered the opening itself.

Tokyo's Nojima robot showroom runs a hands-on trial corner for three camera/AI-glasses crowdfunding projects through October 31.

Fashion designer Zandra Rhodes' complete 57-year design-process archive is on exhibit at De Montfort University through January 16.

The "Archive Unfolded" show draws on a 1964–2021 collection of mock-ups, finished garments, sketches, fabric samples and costing documents that Rhodes donated specifically "to be used for teaching the next generation of fashion designers." It's a concrete, dated instance of process records — not just finished work — entering an institutional collection, a pattern that keeps surfacing well outside hardware or software design circles. A TU Delft thesis called FilaLoom modifies an FDM 3D printer to lay yarn between printed layers, fusing machine fabrication with hand-weaving.

Amsterdam designer Joram de Haas built the hybrid printer for his Master's thesis in Integrated Product Design and has just published a paper on it with TU Delft staff. It's a working precedent for blending machine process with hand-craft at the component level, rather than treating the two as separate stages. Single-source; no second outlet has picked it up yet.

Yesterday's deep dive

A closer look at Singapore-based Lingverse found its "Singapore" identity is a seven-month-old shell over a decade-old Beijing operating business, and the reported "$29M" raise is actually an RMB 200M round closed in China in November 2025, backed by investors named only in Chinese press. The confusion over what happens to a captured photo resolves into four separate steps: the device captures a still, deletes the raw image, and keeps only an AI-generated reinterpretation of it — closer to generating a memory than preserving one. Looking at the wider ambient-capture-wearable category: the "cheap hardware plus subscription" framing doesn't hold for most of it, hardly anyone in the category sells the original record as the product, and three of the four companies that shipped a device like this in volume before 2025 were gone within 22 months. No capture-indicator standard or legal mandate exists anywhere for this class of device — California's SB 1130 was vetoed on September 30 — and indicator hardware and data-retention commitments turn out to be anti-correlated across the whole category.
