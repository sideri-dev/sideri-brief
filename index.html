A new benchmark shows pure visual-similarity search fails on real personal photo albums.

Built from authentic personal photo albums rather than synthetic data, the benchmark finds that "unified embedding models collapse on non-visual constraints" — the best embedding-only model manages just 58% recall at 10, against a system that also reasons over metadata like GPS and timestamps. It's evidence for combining multiple signals rather than relying on image similarity alone in an on-device retrieval system, though the paper's own proposed fix — an agentic system that reasons and selects on the user's behalf — cuts against a design built around retrieval that answers but never selects.

Yesterday's deep-dive

A deep-dive into 18650 battery holders found that nobody sells the part a battery-bay design would assume exists: a holder with a built-in thermistor for surface temperature sensing. A full sweep of major holder catalogs (Keystone, MPD, Würth) found zero mentions of thermistor or NTC anywhere. The industry's three real answers: sense inside a custom-built bay (rare, and the one example disclaims its own accuracy), seal the cell in permanently, or switch to a proprietary branded cell with the sensor already built in. The actual governing safety standard turned out to be one rarely cited in this context (IEC 62368-1, rather than the more commonly referenced JEITA clause), and the real cell-fit tolerance problem turns out to be diameter, not length.
