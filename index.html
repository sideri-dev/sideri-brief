Raspberry Pi 6 is delayed to 2028 at the earliest — and it will ship with no NPU.

Eben Upton's own Reddit AMA, confirmed across two English outlets and independently framed by Japanese tech press, makes clear this isn't a stopgap: the next Pi generation deliberately skips dedicated AI silicon in favor of raw CPU performance, Upton arguing fast, affordable CPUs beat a bolted-on accelerator as the path to democratized AI.

That closes an open question for us — the Pi 5 isn't a placeholder while we wait for a faster NPU-equipped board, it's the platform we're building on for the foreseeable future, and on-device inference needs to be designed around CPU-only compute as a permanent constraint, not a near-term gap.

A well-funded rival is building exactly what we refuse to build: an AI that ranks and retrieves your visual memories.

Memories.ai just closed an $8M seed extension (total $16M) led by Susa Ventures, with Samsung Next and NVIDIA's Cosmos-Reason/Metropolis teams as partners, to build a cloud "Large Visual Memory Model" that actively searches and surfaces moments from a personal capture archive.

It's the sharpest funded counter-example yet to our own approach — search that never ranks, staying entirely on-device — real capital chasing the opposite bet. The founders come out of Meta's Ray-Ban glasses AI team, which says something about where that talent pool thinks the opportunity is.

Meta's 2025 purchase of Limitless (formerly Rewind) is the clearest sign yet that standalone passive-capture wearables don't survive as independent businesses.

Limitless has stopped selling its hardware outright, moved existing customers to a free plan, and wound down a company that had raised $33M from a16z, First Round and NEA.

It's direct precedent for our own device category — two of the most visible funded entrants in passive-capture wearables, Limitless and Amazon's Bee, have both had their hardware businesses either discontinued or absorbed into a platform owner within about a year of raising real money.

A new zero-knowledge scheme lets a photo prove facts about itself without revealing the photo.

A single arXiv preprint describes "soft redaction" — zero-knowledge proofs that certify properties of a capture (proximity to a location, similarity to a known identity) without exposing the underlying sensitive data, constructed in seconds and verified in milliseconds.

It's new ground against our existing work on capture provenance, and speaks directly to the unresolved tension in always-capturing hardware: proving something happened without handing over everything that happened. Single-author preprint, unreplicated — a design-space pointer, not a shipping technique yet.

Yesterday's deep-dive

An earlier deep-dive corrected an earlier read of Raspberry Pi OS's September release rather than confirming it: a claim about the release's reported kernel version turned out to sit at primary in Raspberry Pi's own release notes, not single-sourced as first thought. The September update turned out to be a desktop-layer release almost entirely — a minimal, headless build gets almost none of its headline changes, while the handful of changes that do reach that kind of build appear in no release note at all.

The kernel's projected end-of-life rests on a page stamped seven months stale, though independent signals agree no successor base is imminent. Two candidate Debian bases turn out closer than assumed in supported lifespan — about six weeks apart, not years — but the older option's underlying software froze over a year ago, which for a camera-driven product means an outdated camera-stack pairing not previously flagged. Net effect: the platform base is stable and well-supported well into next year, but an appliance-versus-desktop distinction in Raspberry Pi's own release process had been read incorrectly until now.
