Today

Espressif ships ESP-IDF v6.1-beta1 — the first beta past the v6.0 baseline we build against.

The release brings a JPEG-decoder security fix hardening against malformed-picture attacks, moves ESP-MQTT out of the core repo into the component manager (it's now a separate espressif/mqtt dependency you add yourself), and renames a batch of GPIO and LCD-driver fields — including the color_space/rgb_endian pair, now consolidated into rgb_ele_order. None of it is dramatic alone, but it's exactly the pre-1.0 API churn worth absorbing now, while our firmware is still soft, rather than discovering it mid-integration. Espressif's own roadmap puts rc1 at July 23 and a full rc at July 31, so the v6.1 line is moving quickly.

Single-source: both the release notes and the roadmap are Espressif's own channels, not independent publishers.

Japan's PSE battery-charger standard J60335-2-29(2023) has apparently already passed its transition deadline — on one source only.

J60335-2-29 governs PSE safety requirements for household battery chargers, and per this blog post the grace period for the pre-2023 version ended July 31, 2026 — meaning only the fully 2023-harmonized version should now be valid for new PSE filings. For any device carrying onboard battery-charging circuitry, that's a real design constraint: building against the superseded 2019 spec would force rework before certification. The catch is that this rests on a single Japanese blog. Attempts to corroborate against METI's own 別表第十二 page and japan.ul.com both returned 403, and koukusu.com doesn't list the standard at all — so the July 31 date is provisional, not settled, and it's the target for tonight's deeper dig.

Japan may also loosen 技適 self-certification for small manufacturers — but it's still only a proposal.

A Japanese regulatory-filing site reports that expanding the 自己確認制度 (self-certification system) is 検討されています — "under consideration" — as a way to ease market entry for small manufacturers and startups. This is separate from the OTA/same-number reform an earlier deep-dive already covered; Nikkei's corroboration confirms only the OTA mechanism, not this. If enacted, it would cut into the ¥440,000–550,000 TELEC certification line that same deep-dive established as the realistic cost baseline. But the page itself discloses that its content reflects March 2026 discussion status and may differ from what's implemented — a watch item, not something to plan around.

Espressif's next tier, the ESP32-S31, is now in mass production.

A dual-core 320MHz RISC-V SoC with a SIMD core (~2195 CoreMark), 512KB SRAM, 250MHz DDR PSRAM, up to 60 GPIOs, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.4, 802.15.4, a Gigabit Ethernet MAC and native Matter over Wi-Fi/Thread — corroborated across Espressif's own announcement plus Adafruit, LinuxGizmos, CNX-Software and Hackaday, with specs matching exactly. It sits a tier above the ESP32-S3 our platform runs on, and only becomes relevant if the roadmap ever needs smart-home interoperability or heavier on-device inference than the S3 can carry. The S3 stays the right call — file the S31 as a future-generation migration watch, not an action item.

Yesterday's dossier — Teenage Engineering

Teenage Engineering is far smaller than its cultural footprint suggests — around 90 people, roughly $40M at peak revenue, founder-minority-owned — and nearly died in 2015–16 before a chairman's rescue convertible. Its two near-death product events were both single-vendor component EOL failures, while the product that doubled revenue, the EP-133 K.O. II, was designed procurement-first around parts already in hand, on an in-house-instrumented line in Barcelona. After fourteen years of only-upward pricing, TE has now run two consecutive summers of deep portfolio-wide discounting, coinciding with FY2025 operating profit collapsing from 43.3 to 3.5 MSEK. What actually breaks at that scale isn't the design — it's sourcing concentration and process detail (unpadded packaging, ungloved assembly), both fixed with mechanisms rather than intentions.
