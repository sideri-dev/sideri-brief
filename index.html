Today

The EU's own reporting deadline for connected-device vulnerabilities lands in nine days.

The European Commission's Cyber Resilience Act reporting page confirms that from 11 September 2026, manufacturers of "products with digital elements" must report actively exploited vulnerabilities within 24 hours of becoming aware, with a full notification due within 72 hours. This is an early-tranche obligation, arriving well over a year before the Act's full applicability on 11 December 2027 — the vulnerability-monitoring and incident-reporting infrastructure a connected device needs turns out to be a much earlier build item than the regulation's headline date suggests.

The ESP32-S31's DigiKey listing is finally readable — and it isn't in stock yet.

After several cycles of a Cloudflare block on this exact product page, the ESP32-S31-WROOM-3-N16R16V listing came through today: "New Product" status, priced $7.30 at one piece, 16MB flash / 320kB ROM / 544kB SRAM, rated -40°C to 85°C. Stock reads zero today, with DigiKey's own estimate putting 2,500 units in stock by 27 January 2027 — a live read on how close this chip is to being production-buyable in volume.

Japan's next IoT security-label tier just published, and its queue is backing up.

Japan's Information-technology Promotion Agency (IPA) has published ★3-level security criteria for communication equipment and network cameras under its JC-STAR labeling scheme, alongside a notice that a surge in applications is slowing review times. JC-STAR is a voluntary self-declaration security label sitting alongside Japan's existing radio-certification regime — a live data point on both the bar and the timeline for anyone weighing that label for a Japan launch.

Amazon just hiked the Echo Dot 60% overnight and named the memory shortage directly.

Amazon's own statement: the Echo Dot's price "jumped 60% overnight, from $49.99 to $79.99," attributed to "significant increases in memory and storage component costs" after the company says it absorbed the shock as long as it could — press coverage is calling the broader shortage "RAMmageddon," with prices expected to keep climbing through 2027 before potentially stabilizing in 2028. It's the sharpest hardware-retail data point yet for a component shock that's now hit game consoles, laptops and smart speakers within weeks of each other.

A single sensor module now reads nine different things about the air in one 55mm part.

Sensirion's new SEN68 combines four particulate-matter measurements, temperature, humidity, VOC index, NOx index and formaldehyde sensing into one 55.2×25.6×21.3mm module, drawing about 75mA. It's a useful marker for how much environmental-sensing capability now fits into a single, board-mountable part.

Yesterday's deep-dive

A closer read of yesterday's chip-security advisory found the opposite of what the headline suggested: the flaw it describes explicitly does not affect the firmware-update verification path most devices actually rely on, and no rule in force anywhere in the launch markets under discussion — European, Japanese, or Southeast Asian — actually requires the specific security feature in question; they require verified updates, a related but different and already-satisfiable bar. The real time-pressure item turned out to be a one-way hardware fuse that gets permanently set at factory test and can never be changed afterward, unlike a related protection that can be hardened later in the field. Whether a newer chip revision is even exposed to the underlying bug in the first place remains an open contradiction in the evidence.
