Yesterday's dossier

A deep-dive on a well-known design-led hardware brand found it's far smaller than its cultural footprint suggests, and nearly failed a decade ago before a rescue investment. Its two closest calls since were both tied to relying on a single component supplier, while its biggest recent hit was designed the opposite way — built around parts already available, on a tightly instrumented production line. After years of only-raising prices, the brand has now run two straight years of deep discounting, coinciding with a sharp profit decline in its most recent filed year. The lesson: what breaks at that kind of scale usually isn't the design itself, but sourcing concentration and process details — both fixable with better mechanisms, not just better intentions.
