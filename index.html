Raspberry Pi's camera supplier now ships the Camera Module 3 sensor's identity memory pre-written

A newly published change notice from Raspberry Pi says the factory that assembles the IMX708 image sensor used in the Camera Module 3 will now burn in the sensor's one-time-programmable memory itself, rather than shipping it blank for the buyer to program. Practically, that removes a manufacturing step for anyone building on this camera, and the notice hands over the exact terminal command for checking which sensor variant is installed (dmesg | fgrep -i imx708, with suffix codes for each version) — a useful bring-up and quality-check detail for anyone building hardware on this sensor, including us, on the Raspberry Pi 5 camera stack we're building on. Vendor's own change notice; no second source exists or is expected for this kind of document.

A Tokyo gallery show opening November 13 puts a design studio's process on display instead of its finished objects

AXIS Gallery in Roppongi is hosting "One to one," co-hosted with Foster + Partners' industrial design studio, covering the studio's full range of work from furniture to turbines. The curatorial description is explicit about the angle: the show focuses not on the finished pieces but on the relationships and process that produced them — prototyping, testing, digital modeling, iterated — described in the studio's own words as "design by making." Free admission, November 13–29, sponsored by Okamura and Karimoku with a British Council grant. A real-world precedent for showing process as the content itself, rather than the finished object — the same bet we're making at device scale rather than on a gallery wall. Single source; no second outlet has covered the show yet.

Yesterday's deep dive

Yesterday we took apart Nothing's Phone (4b) launch in Japan and corrected three things the initial read got wrong: it isn't a carrier exclusive (one retailer's own page reserves that word for other Nothing models; the 4b is sold at several other retailers too), the small price gap between the carrier and Nothing's own store is an ordinary rounding step rather than a subsidy, and a mid-2026 report that Nothing was exiting the Japanese market is refuted by paperwork, not denials — Nothing filed for Japanese radio certification on this exact phone 47 days before that exit report ran. What does hold up on inspection: Nothing's entry-level Japan pricing has risen 79% in nine months, and FeliCa support — a real cost, around ¥600,000 per model and non-refundable — was called "a per-model judgement call" by the company's own Japan lead just two days after launch, alongside its Tokyo flagship store slipping back to an undated opening. No exit, but real softening in how the brand is positioned there.
