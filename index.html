Espressif published its own worked example of what it actually takes to pass the EU's mandatory radio-equipment cybersecurity standard.

EN 18031 has been mandatory for RED-covered radio devices since August 1, 2025, and Espressif just walked through a full assessment on the ESP32-C5 running its RainMaker cloud stack — stating the same security architecture, and therefore the same finding, extends to the C3 and C6. The requirements checklist is concrete: Secure Boot and signed OTA, TLS-authenticated communication, eFUSE/flash encryption, account and data deletion, event notification, remote diagnostics. It's the clearest primary-source map yet of what an EU-bound Wi-Fi device on this chip family would actually need to clear.

A wholesaler's sudden, unexplained 1990s closure nearly erased a hand-woven horsehair mesh that fine Japanese cooking depends on.

AXIS profiles the straining tool used for pastes and removing lumps in delicate Japanese dishes: a master craftsman sourced the horsehair mesh from a wholesaler for years, until one day that wholesaler announced closure and refused to say who had actually been weaving the material. The technique died out entirely until Mifuki Okawa tracked the original loom to a textile museum in Takamatsu and revived it by hand — a vivid, documented case of a single specialized supplier vanishing overnight with no visible upstream chain.

Yesterday's deep-dive

A closer look at Pebble Round 2's manufacturing hiccup found the widely-quoted "about 2 months" was actually the build time for 14,000 watches once mass production starts, not the recovery time from the cosmetic defect that blocked it in the first place — the real recovery window looks more like 3.5 to 4 months from the original production halt, and no source from the maker yet confirms mass production has actually restarted. Earlier coverage that seemed to disagree about the cause turned out to be a translation artifact, not a real contradiction. The most durable finding outlives the schedule entirely: no published standard governs cosmetic acceptance of a machined or moulded metal part. What actually governs is a company-written criteria chart and a physically signed limit sample, and suppliers will quote new-tool lead times freely while treating a tooling fix as a case-by-case conversation — meaning a late cosmetic defect can't be schedule-budgeted from public information at all; it has to be quoted directly.
