Japan's radio regulator just formally ratified the certification reform this platform's approval path already assumes — and it comes with a concrete firmware requirement.

The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' Telecommunications Council (情報通信審議会) issued its first full interim report on its wireless-equipment certification review, escalating a working-group draft (comment period closed in June) to Council-level status — the step right before the ministerial ordinance actually changes. The core reform: a device can add a software-version field to its existing certification number and keep that same number across firmware updates, as long as the current software version is visible on the device's own management screen. That's no longer a proposal — it's now a firmware/UI requirement to design against. The report also notes roughly 21,000 certification filings last fiscal year, about 45% obtained through European or US certification bodies, and flags that module-embedded certification can leave the compliance mark unconfirmable on a finished product.

Bosch's BME690 is a drop-in replacement for the environmental gas sensor already in this build, at roughly half the power draw.

Same 3×3×0.93mm³ footprint as the BME688, pin-compatible, with power consumption "slashed... by up to 50%." Bosch's own marketing names food-freshness detection and kitchen rangehoods as target applications directly. One correction worth flagging: this is a two-year-old announcement (June 2024), not new — it surfaced via a distributor stocking date that made it look current. Age aside, it's never come up before and it's a real candidate worth evaluating against the current part.

Melexis has a thermal-imaging sensor at the exact resolution this platform already uses, with a shutter type built for a moving hand — and their own marketing already says "smart cooking."

The MLX90642 matches the MLX90640's 32×24 resolution but reads with a global shutter instead of a rolling one, which cuts motion artifacts when the camera or the subject in front of it is moving — exactly the pan-and-hand scenario a kitchen thermal sensor sits in. Noise-equivalent temperature difference is 65mK at a 2Hz refresh rate, configurable up to 16Hz, over the same I2C interface as its predecessor. About 16 months old and honestly dated by Melexis, never covered here before — a real upgrade path worth weighing.

GoPro is in "later stages" of a sale process, and the number worth remembering is that its owned online channel grew while retail nearly halved.

Units shipped down 38% year-over-year, revenue down 31%, cash down to $27.3 million — a genuine going-concern quarter. But inside that collapse: retail-channel sales fell 48% to $58 million while direct-to-consumer sales grew 13% to $47 million, now 44% of revenue, alongside a record 69% accessory-attach rate on camera sales. It's a live stress test of the direct-to-consumer-first channel bet a lot of small hardware makers are now making — even a near-failure quarter didn't break the owned-channel growth.

MIT put a number on how good portable smell-classification can actually get right now: 63.3%.

The SmellNet dataset, presented at ICLR 2026 by a team including MIT Media Lab's Paul Pu Liang, covers 50 substances and 43 mixtures across roughly 828,000 time-series data points. The best model in the paper hits 63.3% top-1 accuracy when trained with lab-grade chemical-analysis supervision, dropping to around 50% under a stricter evaluation threshold. That's the honest external ceiling for what a portable volatile-compound sensor can discriminate today — a useful sanity check for anyone scoping how far a small gas-sensing chip can be pushed toward narrow event classification rather than deep modeling. Single-source (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed).

Yesterday's deep-dive — is the current production chip still the right bet

An earlier deep-dive tested whether the ESP32-S3 still holds up as the production chip against newer alternatives, including Espressif's ESP32-P4 and its C-series RISC-V chips. It found the "this chip is aging out" worry doesn't hold — Espressif's own published support commitment for the S3 runs to January 2033, no discontinuation flag exists, and tens of thousands of units sit in distributor stock. The real churn risk sits with the P4: Espressif already discontinued the original P4 parts just fourteen months into mass production, replacing them with a hardware- and software-incompatible revision, and no radio-capable module for it exists yet. The C-series chips fail on software-tooling support, and the newest S3-successor chip is real but not yet available through any certified, production-grade supply channel. The finding: keep building on the current chip, with the newer one tracked as a future migration candidate.
