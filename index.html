Raspberry Pi's own Japanese retail channel moved on memory pricing before the company did.

Akizuki Denshi's price-history log for the Pi 5 2GB board shows a jump from ¥14,780 to ¥15,180 on September 25 — six days before Raspberry Pi's own global $12.50 hike landed on October 1. The board is currently unorderable there, stock status reading "lead time being confirmed, arrival date undetermined."

It's the clearest sign yet that Japan's distributor channel is pricing in the DRAM shock ahead of the vendor's own announcements, on a board family widely used in single-board-computer projects.

A new paper does, in one pass, roughly the on-device signal a camera-based hand-tracking system needs.

EgoFound3R reconstructs egocentric hand geometry while also outputting point-wise visibility, contact, and distance in the same pass, claiming accuracy gains of 11–43% over prior benchmarks and about 6x the throughput.

It's architecture evidence, not a shipped benchmark — the throughput figure is an offline-processing claim, not a number measured on a Raspberry Pi — but it names the right shape of signal for detecting when a hand occludes the frame.

Yesterday's deep-dive

An earlier deep-dive went back to Raspberry Pi's own regulated financial filing — released seven days before its public price-hike blog post — and found the filing states the cause more plainly: the cheap FY2025 memory inventory that funded the old 2GB price protection has been consumed, gross margin fell to 23%, and meaningful new supply isn't expected until 2028.

The hike also moves the 2GB tier onto the same $16.25-per-GB line as every denser option, leaving only the 1GB board subsidized — and that one is nearly impossible to buy anywhere. One method finding stands out above the price move itself: a "third hike since launch" claim that had been circulating was traced to a fabrication introduced by a search-summarization layer, not by any outlet or primary source.

This week

Memory density on a Raspberry Pi-class capture board has become an irreversible purchase against a moving, lock-protected shelf, and this week's research gave three different buying answers because nobody has actually benchmarked the workload yet. One read favored a mid-size tier based on a stock snapshot that turned out to be a temporary gap; another made the power-and-price case for the smallest tier on paper; a third pointed out the vision encoder has never been benchmarked on this board's CPU at all. The real gating step is running that benchmark before committing to a size.

A week of close tracking on Raspberry Pi's pricing moves produced four lead stories and four same-day corrections — not on the facts quoted, but on how many independent sources actually stood behind each claim. Three stories over-counted corroboration (chains of outlets citing each other back to one forum post or one vendor announcement), one under-counted a primary-sourced claim, and one fabricated detail turned out to originate in a search-summarization layer sitting upstream of the reporting entirely.
