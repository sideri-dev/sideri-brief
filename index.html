Today

Japan's certification reform is real, but the story moved: the comment window already closed, and the mechanism is "same number," not "no review."

Yesterday's lead pointed at an August 4 ministry package; the follow-up deep dive corrected it — that package is unrelated, and the actual software-update reform finished public comment on June 22 and now waits on promulgation, realistically autumn. What it delivers: a radio-relevant firmware update still gets reviewed, but the device keeps its certification number — no re-labeling, no recall — provided the user can see the firmware version on a screen or app.

Two decisions follow now, both free: stay on the pre-certified-module path, where certification transfers whole and the antenna is the only trip-wire, and design in a visible firmware-version readout.

Singapore is the cheap second market: the new three-tier framework routes short-range devices through self-declaration.

IMDA's revised registration regime keeps supplier's declaration of conformity and adds a lightest tier for short-range WiFi/BLE devices — likely reusing existing FCC/CE test data with no new lab work, and even the heavier tier runs 10 working days, not the weeks the trade press claimed. One classification check before hardware lock decides which tier applies.

C-PRAV — IMDA framework update ↗

MIT published the recipe for turning sensor streams into trustworthy narrative.

Rico and Larson show language models fail on raw sensor time-series but become reliably grounded when given statistical summaries, sensor metadata, and context — with evaluator models confirming less hallucination and contradiction. That is our sensor platform's core software problem stated and solved at method level: summarize on-device before any model sees the stream, and the narration stops being inventable.

It is firmware-only work, prototypable on current bench hardware now.
