Vermicular just raised prices again — its second increase in ten weeks.

The Japanese cast-iron cookware maker (Aichi Dobby) confirmed a roughly 3% price rise on its frying pans, oven-safe skillets and yukihira pots effective October 1, citing "recent increases in raw materials, supplies and logistics costs." It's the brand's second distinct price move since early August, when its pot line rose 3–5% — a repeatable pattern rather than a one-off: name the input-cost pressure plainly, move one product line at a time, keep the tone matter-of-fact. A useful template for how a premium hardware maker might justify a price increase against rising component costs without it reading as margin extraction.

A low-power chip just hit 99.68% accuracy detecting cooking activity from ambient gas sensors.

A May 2026 research paper describes a hierarchical clustering method running directly on ESP32 microcontroller hardware that distinguishes cooking from other indoor activity using off-the-shelf machine-learning models, in under 34 microseconds, on low power and memory. It's a close match for a narrow but useful design philosophy: detect and classify one class of event cheaply on small hardware, and leave everything more elaborate to software running above it. Single-source preprint; no newer paper found on this exact combination.

Yesterday's deep-dive

A deep-dive into a Raspberry Pi 5 storage-corruption bug found it real and unusually stubborn: a two-argument swap in the NVMe storage driver shipped in a longterm kernel line, was never tagged for the stable-release backport process, and took 236 days to reach the official long-term-support branch — an official operating-system image carried it silently for 89 of those days, and the default update path still carries it today. On this hardware's architecture the defect can only corrupt data being read back, not data already written, so files on disk survive intact — but anything computed from a bad read and then saved permanently, such as a cryptographic signature over a photo, would be permanently wrong with nothing in the stack able to detect it afterward. One open gap the deep-dive surfaced: no periodic data-integrity-verification practice exists yet for whichever storage approach eventually gets used on this class of hardware.
