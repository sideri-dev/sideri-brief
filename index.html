Raspberry Pi OS's September update adds an icon dock — but a widely cited "kernel 6.18.50 LTS" figure traces to only one outlet, not Raspberry Pi's own post

Raspberry Pi's own blog confirms a new icon dock that can replace or sit alongside the taskbar, plus a combined icon-based launcher and task list. A kernel version number and a "moves to Debian 13 Trixie" framing that circulated alongside the dock news don't appear on Raspberry Pi's own page at all — they trace to a single outlet, and the "Trixie migration" claim is flat wrong: this is a desktop update on the OS's existing Trixie base, not a new migration.

We're building a background process for this exact OS, so the real kernel and support-window state matters to us more than the headline feature — worth a closer look before anything gets built against it.

https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/an-updated-look-for-the-raspberry-pi-desktop/

Framework paired a retroactive memory-price cut with a public pledge not to profiteer off the DRAM shortage

Framework secured Micron LPCAMM2 memory modules at a lower cost, cut its DIY and pre-built pricing accordingly, and is refunding the difference to customers who already ordered. The line worth keeping: "We won't use this as an excuse to be extractive. We'll only increase pricing to cover increases in our costs, and where possible, we'll absorb costs to maintain stability."

It's a clean example of a premium, repairable-by-design hardware brand narrating a component-cost shock to direct buyers without eroding trust — the same memory squeeze that's been showing up in HP, Lenovo and Raspberry Pi's own numbers all year, and worth studying for how we'd talk about the same kind of squeeze ourselves.

https://frame.work/blog/updates-on-memory-pricing-and-navigating-the-volatile-memory-market

A new paper puts real numbers on how far local, on-device search can scale before it degrades

"As We May Search" reports dense retrieval holding above 91% accuracy (nDCG@10) out to 100,000 documents, with approximate indexing stretching that to a million documents at only a 2% quality loss — a 7-billion-parameter local language model lands within 4 points of a cloud-based baseline on answer quality.

That's real support for a bet we're making: keeping search entirely on-device rather than routing it through a cloud service. An index built this way doesn't quietly degrade well past the size a personal archive built over years would realistically reach.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29652

Nothing's Headphone (1) Pro is officially out in Japan at ¥64,800, with a confirmed 68-hour battery figure beating the earlier leak

Nothing's Japan press release confirms an aluminum-and-titanium chassis at 327 grams, and GIGAZINE adds that the headband arm is CNC-machined from a single block of aluminum, 45% lighter than the original Headphone (1). The official battery figure — 68 hours with noise cancelling off — replaces the 60-hour number that had been circulating as a leak just three days earlier.

We keep a close eye on Nothing's Japan business as a bellwether for premium consumer hardware in that market, and this is a live example of how a design-forward brand talks about materials and weight reduction at launch.

Worth reading next to the Japan launch above — the same company running two different channel strategies in two different markets at once, which is exactly the kind of contrast we watch for as we think about our own distribution approach.

Yesterday's deep-dive

A Raspberry Pi engineer confirmed on the company's own forum that boards lock to their factory RAM size, with additional undisclosed "device attributes" now programmed in — and that statement is the entire vendor record; it appears in no product-change notice, datasheet, release note or register page. The corroborating sources behind the original story collapse to roughly one, since a widely cited follow-up post is a transcript of its own video pointing back at the same forum thread, and three of the original claims turned out wrong: the actual failure mode is a board that boots normally and reports the factory size, not one that won't boot at all; an early-generation-board claim is unestablished; and our own fallback board is a clean negative on three independent grounds.

The finding that actually matters for what we're building isn't the lock itself but its collision with the supply channel — two RAM tiers of the board we're targeting are simultaneously unbuyable at every authorised Japanese reseller right now, and the only channel with a stocked, certified board is the unauthorised one, at a 13–19% premium.
